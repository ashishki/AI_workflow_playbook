# Grow

Treat growth as a new question, not a promise of automatic scalability. Determine
what changed: concurrent users, roles, data volume, rule complexity, availability,
responsibility or spending. Understand current observed limits and actual demand.

Check ownership and approval in the organization, account isolation, access changes,
concurrent writes, backup/restore, monitoring, support responsibilities and budget.
Compare keeping the current solution, configuring a supported service and engineering
changes. Do not introduce a new database/platform merely for future flexibility.

Design and test the specific growth boundary with representative load and failure
cases. Local personal use is not evidence of multi-user safety. Do not weaken existing
privacy restrictions because a manager wants to share. Involve a qualified specialist
when required; explain the handoff instead of selling unlimited DIY support.

Deliver the supported scale/conditions, needed changes, cost/support consequences,
rollback and explicit untested limits. Return to Design/Change and Verify. No invented
enterprise-ready, compliance certification or unlimited-user claim.
